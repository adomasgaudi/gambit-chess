What gzip thinks the chess openings are

No engine, no evaluation, no chess knowledge whatsoever. Each opening is just its main line written in algebraic notation. If two lines compress better together than apart, they share structure — and that alone is enough to rediscover the opening families.

NCD(x, y) = ( C(xy) − min(C(x), C(y)) ) / max(C(x), C(y))   C = gzip -9, bytes

The similarity graph

Every pair of openings is scored, then each opening keeps its 2 nearest neighbours. Colours are not assigned — they come from label propagation over those edges, so each colour is a family the compressor found on its own. Shorter, darker edges mean lower distance. Hover a node for its line.

What it got right, and what it got wrong

Method borrowed verbatim from the idea-similarity graph in the see research project: gzip NCD, k-nearest-neighbour edges, label propagation, weight 1 − NCD. Only the corpus changed — ideas out, move lines in. Lines are 6 moves of the main variation, uniform length so no opening gets a compression advantage from being longer.
